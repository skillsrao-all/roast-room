## Why the council landed here

- **Retail and food loses money on every drop.** Drops cost $15 to $25 against a needed $8 to $12, and Wing with Walmart, Zipline and Amazon already own the merchants. The $750k is about 1,000 times short of Zipline's latest raise. Every seat scored B at 1. (Hormozi, LTV to CAC; Buffett, great, good and gruesome)
- **Nobody pays for a software middle layer.** The operators that matter build their own dispatch, and a layer on top of their customers is the first thing they would copy or close off. (Bezos, undifferentiated heavy lifting; Buffett, economic castle)
- **The health system buys the outcome, not a tool.** At an assumed $50 a run with $25 to the operator, one busy hospital is worth about nine times a software seat. (Hormozi, Value Equation)
- **Approval is the bottleneck, and you can rent it.** New areas take 12 to 24 months to approve, which is most of the runway. Subcontracting an operator already approved for a corridor skips that wait. (Elon, work the limiting factor)

## Where they fought

**Is there a buyer for software between customers and operators?** Round 1 was unanimous for D. Bezos and Buffett were asked to argue that no buyer exists, and they convinced themselves. Graham, Hormozi and Elon followed. Resolved: no buyer at volume.

**Sell software, or sell the delivery?** Hormozi and Elon argued the managed service from the outcome side and from first principles. Four seats moved from D to A. Resolved: sell the delivery.

**What if the founder's contacts are in retail?** Graham and Codie argued that a warm retail buyer beats a medical market with no way in. Hormozi, Buffett and Elon said retail contacts point to waiting, not to retail software, because the trips lose money. Not resolved. Every seat flagged that the brief never says where the contacts are.
