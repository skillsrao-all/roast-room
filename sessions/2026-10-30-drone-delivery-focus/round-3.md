AMENDMENT OR DISSENT: In condition 3, replace the $50 floor with the 60% ratio from condition 4. ## Chair

All six seats endorsed with an amendment. No dissent.

1. **Bezos: replace the fixed $50 floor with the 60% cost ratio.** Adopted. $50 stays the asking price, but a pilot passes if the operator's cost is at or under 60% of the price. Hormozi's 30-day cash test (below) now guards the economics, so a fixed floor is no longer needed. No seat argued that $50 itself is required.
2. **Graham: runs per week must grow every week by month 6, or A is failing.** Adopted. No seat objected, and it pairs with Elon's weekly metric.
3. **Hormozi: first 30 days of pilot cash over 2 times the cost to win and serve the customer, before signing hospital two.** Adopted. No seat objected.
4. **Buffett: the startup carries its own chain-of-custody liability policy, and the operator subcontract indemnifies it, before the first paid run.** Adopted. Bezos, Hormozi and Elon each raised the same liability risk.
5. **Elon: at least 20 runs a week per pilot, reviewed weekly.** Adopted. No seat objected.
6. **Codie, part one: by day 30, price out buying a regional ground medical courier on a seller note under $300k.** Adopted as a check, not a commitment. It is cheap to price and no seat argued against it.
7. **Codie, part two: if the contacts are in retail, put the merchant aggregator back on the table instead of defaulting to E.** Not adopted. In Round 2, Hormozi, Buffett and Elon argued that the networks already own the merchant and that retail trips lose money, and Codie's own Round 2 card conceded the loss on retail drops. It goes to the minority report.
