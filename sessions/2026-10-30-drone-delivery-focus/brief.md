# Brief: Which drone delivery use case to focus on

**The decision:** A new startup wants to enter US drone delivery in 2026. Which use case should it focus on, if any?

**Options**
- **A. Medical and healthcare logistics.** Lab samples, pharmacy, and hospital-to-hospital runs, sold as B2B contracts to health systems.
- **B. Suburban retail and food last mile.** Consumer orders from stores and restaurants, in the market where Wing with Walmart, Zipline, and Amazon Prime Air already fly.
- **C. Industrial B2B niche.** Parts and tools to job sites, utilities, agriculture, and remote sites.
- **D. Picks-and-shovels software or infrastructure.** Dispatch, order integration, compliance, or landing-site software sold to operators and retailers. No aircraft of its own.
- **E. Don't start yet.** Wait for the FAA's Part 108 routine-flight rule, then re-decide.

**The business:** Pre-revenue, no product yet. Has some funding: about $750k (assumed). Founding team of 2 to 3 (assumed). The founder's edge is industry access (contacts in a target industry) and the ability to build software. No aircraft or aviation engineering background.

**The market (researched, October 2026)**
- Wing with Walmart is targeting 60 million US households by the end of 2026, across about 150 stores. Walmart passed 1 million drone deliveries in May 2026.
- Zipline passed 2 million cumulative deliveries in January 2026 and raised $600M that month. Amazon Prime Air flies in five states and announced metro Atlanta in May 2026.
- Drone deliveries cost about $15 to $25 each today. Margins at scale need $8 to $12. Networks average 3 to 8 deliveries per drone per day; the economics need far more.
- The FAA's Part 108 rule, which would replace case-by-case waivers for flights beyond the pilot's line of sight, went to White House review on July 10, 2026. It was still unpublished in mid-October. Until it lands, operators fly under Part 135 certificates or exemptions, and new areas can take 12 to 24 months to approve. A separate TSA security framework is still pending.
- Medical deliveries support premium pricing. Industrial and document runs earn high margins per trip but have limited scale.

**Resources:** About $750k (assumed), 18 to 24 months of runway (assumed). Founder full time.

**Success:** Paying customers and a repeatable revenue line within 12 months; a business that can raise a seed round within 18 months (assumed).

**Lean:** Retail and food delivery (Option B), because it is the biggest and most visible market (reason assumed). The council should test this lean, not echo it.
